How to practice debating with AI

A good argument has a clear claim, a reason, an example and a response to the other side. Use these three short exercises before trying an AI debate app.

A five-minute practice routine

  1. Choose a position. Even if you disagree with it, try to explain it fairly.
  2. Make a claim. Write one sentence stating what should change.
  3. Give a reason and example. Explain why the claim matters and when it applies.
  4. Anticipate an objection. Write the strongest reasonable response from the other side.
  5. Rebut and revise. Answer that objection without attacking the person holding it.

Three practice topics

1. Should schools allow phones during class?

Try the for side by arguing for responsible access in emergencies. Then try the against side by arguing for attention and fewer distractions. Include one exception in your rebuttal.

2. Is a four-day working week better?

Separate employee wellbeing from business coverage. Compare a small office with a 24-hour service team. A useful rebuttal acknowledges that a single schedule will not fit every job.

3. Should AI-generated images be clearly labeled?

Discuss transparency and creative freedom. Consider what changes when an image is used as entertainment versus evidence in a news story. Define what counts as a meaningful label.

A simple self-review checklist

LogicDoes the conclusion follow from reasons, and did you address an important exception?
PersuasionDid you give a concrete example relevant to someone who disagrees?
RebuttalDid you answer the other side's strongest point rather than a weaker version?

These are practice criteria, not official scores or guarantees of better performance. AI feedback can be incomplete; verify factual claims independently.

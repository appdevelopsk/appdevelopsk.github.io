How to take the photo

Stand square to the wall — directly in front of what you are checking, not off to one side — and fill the frame with the edge itself. HangTrue reads the angle in the photo, so if you shoot from an angle, perspective tilts the edge and the reading tilts with it.

What the verdicts mean

Under 0.3° is true: that is roughly 5 mm out across a metre, which is the point at which people stop noticing. Between 0.3° and 1° is slightly out — visible if you look for it. Over 1° is visibly crooked.

The millimetre figure

An angle is hard to act on, so HangTrue converts it. Set the slider to how wide the object is — the distance between the two ends you would actually adjust — and it tells you how far one end has to move. A 3° tilt across a 600 mm frame is about 34 mm.

Level or Plumb

Use Level for anything that should run horizontally: pictures, shelves, blinds, worktops. Use Plumb for anything that should run vertically: door frames, posts, wallpaper seams, the uprights of a bookcase.

It says there are no clear straight edges

The edge needs to stand out from what is behind it. A white frame on a white wall in flat light gives the app very little to measure. Get closer, or shoot it where the light gives the edge some contrast.

It says nothing is near level

Everything strong in the photo is running more than 20° away from the reference you chose. Either the phone was held at an angle, or you want the other reference — try Plumb instead of Level, or the reverse.

The reading is marked as rough

When the edges in a photo disagree with each other — a busy scene, several objects at different angles — HangTrue says so rather than presenting a confident number it cannot support. A closer shot of one long edge will give a firmer answer.

Does it change my photo?

No. HangTrue only reads. It does not edit, crop, save, copy or upload anything, and it never writes to your photo library.
